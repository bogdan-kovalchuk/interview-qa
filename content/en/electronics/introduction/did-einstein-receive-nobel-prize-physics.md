## Short answer

Einstein received the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics for his services to theoretical physics, and especially for his discovery of the law of the photoelectric effect; the award was actually presented in 1922.[^nobel-einstein] It was not awarded for the theory of relativity.

## Detailed explanation

Einstein was recognized for his services to theoretical physics, primarily for the law of the photoelectric effect, not for the theory of relativity.[^nobel-einstein]

In the photoelectric effect, light striking a material can transfer energy to electrons and release them from the surface. For a single photon, energy depends on the frequency of the light: if it is below the threshold for the material, simply increasing the brightness will not eject electrons; a higher frequency can do so. This explanation was pivotal for the development of quantum physics.[^nobel-speech]

The award citation should not be reduced to claiming that Einstein "invented solar panels". The photoelectric effect underlies certain methods of converting light, but the Nobel citation concerns a physical law, not a specific device.[^nobel-einstein]

**Common mistakes:**

- Attributing the prize to the theory of relativity: this is a widespread belief, but it does not match the official Nobel Prize citation.[^nobel-einstein]
- Stating that any light ejects electrons regardless of frequency; there is a threshold determined by the material.[^nobel-speech]

**Example:** the prize was awarded for 1921, but it was announced and presented to Einstein in 1922. These two dates describe the prize year and the ceremony, respectively.[^nobel-einstein]
