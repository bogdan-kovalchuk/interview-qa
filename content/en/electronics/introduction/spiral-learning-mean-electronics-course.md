## Short answer

Spiral learning means returning to key topics multiple times, each time adding complexity and building upon what has already been learned. In an electronics course, Ohm's law might first appear in a simple resistive circuit, and later – in a circuit with a diode or transistor.[^edinburgh-spiral-curriculum]

## Detailed explanation

Spiral learning is an approach to course design in which key concepts reappear in new contexts and at increasingly complex levels. Repetition here does not mean going through the same lesson verbatim: each subsequent iteration must use previously acquired ideas and expand upon them.[^edinburgh-spiral-curriculum]

For example, in the first stage, a student might examine a power source, a resistor, and an LED as a simple series circuit. Later, the same concept of current helps analyze a transistor switch, and even later – evaluate multiple branches, voltage drops, component tolerances, or switching behavior. The underlying principle is preserved, but new conditions and applications are introduced.

In such a sequence, new topics are not detached from previous ones. Knowledge of voltage and current becomes a tool for understanding components, and reading a schematic gradually transitions into calculation, assembly, or simulation. It is precisely returning with a new task that can show whether the student is able to transfer a familiar rule rather than just recognize a familiar example. The spiral approach is a way to structure a syllabus; the mere presence of repetition does not guarantee that every topic has been mastered.[^edinburgh-spiral-curriculum]

**Common mistake:**

- Assuming any repetition is spiral learning. If material is repeated without a new level of complexity, context, or connection to what was previously learned, it is merely repetition.

**Example:**

First calculate the LED current with a series resistor, then use that same understanding of current when selecting a base resistor for a transistor, and later explain how that transistor drives a load. Each step brings a familiar idea back into a broader circuit.
