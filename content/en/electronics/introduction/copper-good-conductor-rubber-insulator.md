## Short answer

Copper conducts electricity because solid metal has mobile conduction electrons; in rubber under ordinary conditions, there are far fewer mobile carriers. The difference is explained by the electronic states and energy bands of the material, rather than simply counting the valence electrons of an isolated atom.[^openstax-conductors-insulators][^openstax-band-theory]

## Detailed explanation

Conductivity depends on whether charge can respond to an electric field and move through a material. In copper, conduction electrons in the metal can drift under the influence of an electric field; this does not mean that an electron is free from interactions with the crystal lattice, but rather describes its available states in the solid.[^openstax-conductors-insulators][^openstax-band-theory]

In the band model of a conductor, the highest electron-occupied band is partially filled, so electrons can change states under the influence of an electric field without crossing a large forbidden bandgap. In a typical insulator, the occupied band is full, and the nearest available states are separated by an energy bandgap; ordinary fields are insufficient to create mobile carriers in bulk. It is this model that is more accurate than claiming the conductivity of copper is explained by a single weakly bound electron per atom.[^openstax-band-theory]

Rubber is the name of a class of materials and compounds, so its electrical properties depend on composition, fillers, surface condition, and operating conditions. In typical insulating rubber, carriers move with great difficulty, and the material exhibits high resistivity; contamination or moisture can cause surface leakage, and an excessive electric field can lead to breakdown. Therefore, "insulator" denotes practically low conductivity under specified conditions, rather than an absolute impossibility of current flow.[^openstax-conductors-insulators]

Example: in a cable, the copper core provides a conductive path for current, while the rubber jacket isolates it from humans and other conductors. The jacket reduces leakage current, but its suitability is determined by voltage rating, thickness, temperature, aging, and environment, not by the name "rubber" alone. Conductor and insulation serve different functions in the same product, although both materials can deviate from ideal behavior.[^openstax-conductors-insulators]
