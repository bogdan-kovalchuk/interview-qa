## Short answer

Heat-shrink tubing insulates exposed electrical connections and provides mechanical protection and strain relief for wires and components. Slide the tubing over the wire before making the joint, then position it over the soldered area and heat it according to the manufacturer's specification.[^molex-heat-shrink]

## Detailed explanation

Heat-shrink tubing is a polymeric insulation that shrinks under heat to enclose a wire or spliced joint. It is slid over the wire before the connection is made, then positioned over the joint and heated according to the specific tubing specification. After shrinking, it helps insulate exposed conductors and protects the joint against abrasion and mechanical wear.[^molex-heat-shrink]

Standard single-wall tubing primarily provides electrical insulation, mechanical protection, and wire identification, but it is not a universal sealant. Dual-wall tubing with an adhesive liner can provide a superior environmental seal and strain relief upon heating; the exact degree depends on the material, shrink ratio, and installation conditions.[^molex-heat-shrink]

Before selecting tubing, verify the expanded and recovered diameters, operating temperature range, and material compatibility with the environment. The tubing must slide freely over the largest part of the assembly before shrinking and recover snugly around the smaller dimension. Tubing that is cut too short leaves exposed metal, while overheating can damage the wire insulation or the component itself. It does not replace a mechanical clamp where the cable is subjected to regular, high tensile stress.[^molex-heat-shrink]

For example, for a soldered inline wire splice, select tubing that slides easily over one wire prior to soldering and, after recovery, covers both insulation jackets and the joint itself. Heating must be applied evenly without charring the insulation; obtain the appropriate temperature profile from the manufacturer documentation rather than assuming it is identical across all materials.[^molex-heat-shrink]
