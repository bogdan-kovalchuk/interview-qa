## Short answer

N-type silicon is formed by doping with a donor impurity, such as phosphorus, so electrons become the mobile majority carriers. P-type is formed with an acceptor impurity, such as boron, and holes become the majority carriers. Both types of material are overall electrically neutral; the letters N and P indicate the sign of the majority carriers, not the charge of the overall sample.[^aac-electrons-holes]

## Detailed explanation

N-type and P-type describe a semiconductor in which doping has shifted the ratio of mobile charge carriers. In a silicon crystal, an atom has four valence electrons and forms four covalent bonds with neighbors. A pentavalent impurity, such as phosphorus, occupies a lattice site, forms four bonds, and the fifth electron is weakly bound and can participate in conduction. Such atoms are donors, and electrons are the majority carriers in N-type.[^aac-electrons-holes]

A trivalent impurity, such as boron, has only three electrons for bonds with four neighbors. The missing electron is described as a hole in the valence band. Electrons from neighboring bonds can sequentially transition, filling it, so the hole looks as if it moves through the crystal; this is a convenient description of positive charge transfer, not a separate positive particle. In P-type, holes are the majority, and electrons are the minority.[^aac-electrons-holes]

A sample does not acquire a net charge merely because it became N- or P-type. After donating or accepting electrons, impurity atoms remain charged ionized centers in the lattice that compensate for the charge of mobile carriers. Doping increases the concentration of one carrier type compared to an intrinsic semiconductor, but conductivity also depends on impurity concentration, temperature, material, and carrier mobility.[^aac-electrons-holes]

For example, phosphorus in silicon increases the number of electrons, whereas boron increases the number of holes. This does not mean that current in P-type material is carried only by holes: electrons are also present, but under normal conditions they are the minority. Similarly, in N-type material there are minority holes.[^aac-electrons-holes]

**Common mistakes:**
- Calling N-type material negatively charged and P-type material positively charged. The designations refer to the majority carriers, not the net charge of the sample.
- Imagining a hole as an immobile positive impurity. A hole moves due to the sequential transition of electrons between bonds.
- Claiming that the impurity can only be a single element. Phosphorus is an example of a donor for silicon, and boron is an example of an acceptor; other impurities exist as well.[^aac-electrons-holes]

Thus, the key difference is the type of impurity and the corresponding majority carrier type; both materials remain semiconductors whose conductivity can be adjusted by doping.[^aac-electrons-holes]
