## Short answer

Side cutters and pliers provide controlled cutting and gripping of fine wires and component leads during prototyping; precision electronics flush cutters can cut leads flush against the board. Tool selection depends on wire material and diameter, so cutters should only be operated within their rated specifications.[^facom-417spmt] Cutting edges must match the wire type and gauge rather than being overloaded on hard or oversized materials.[^facom-417spmt]

## Detailed explanation

Side cutters snip wires and component leads, while pliers hold, bend, or position them during prototype assembly. High-quality hand tools feature properly shaped jaws and cutting edges, providing better access in confined areas and delivering repeatable results. For example, manufacturer FACOM specifies compact, slender diagonal cutters designed for clearance between DIP leads on a 0.65 mm pitch and cutting flush; this is a characteristic of a specific model, not every cutter.[^facom-417spmt]

Quality does not mean that a more expensive tool is universally suited to every task. Maximum cut diameter, wire hardness, cut bevel geometry, and physical clearance must all be considered. A tool with overly bulky jaws can nick adjacent pins, while mismatched cutting edges can crush soft copper wire instead of shearing it cleanly. Pliers should not be clamped onto component bodies or fine wires with excessive force, which can easily damage insulation or crack packages.[^facom-417spmt]

Example: after soldering a through-hole component, side cutters trim the protruding lead while the operator holds or shields the cutoff so the loose wire fragment does not fly across the bench. When making a jumper wire, pliers help bend the wire cleanly without stressing the joint or repeatedly fatiguing the lead at the component body. Cutting is performed before applying power, and the board is subsequently inspected to ensure no stray clippings remain to cause short circuits.[^facom-417spmt]

**Common mistakes:** cutting hardened steel or oversized wire with precision cutters designed for fine copper leads, using side cutters as pliers to bend leads, and leaving sharp or jagged lead protrusions. Always check the tool's intended rating and limits before cutting, and inspect the board to clean away stray metal trimmings.
