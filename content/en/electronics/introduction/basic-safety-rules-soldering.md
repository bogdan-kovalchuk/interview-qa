## Short answer

Keep the hot soldering tip in its stand, do not touch it, and use fume extraction to pull flux smoke away from your face.[^hakko-fx888d-safety] [^hse-solder-fume-indg248] Wear eye protection when there is a risk of flying debris or solder splashes.[^osha-eye-protection] Do not overheat the iron, and wash your hands before eating or taking a break if using leaded solder to avoid transferring residues.[^hse-soldering-controls]

## Detailed explanation

A safe soldering workstation minimizes the risk of burns and inhalation of flux fumes. The tip reaches elevated temperatures, so the iron must always be parked in its stand when not in active use. Never leave a powered iron unattended, and route the power cord so that accidental arm movements cannot snag it and pull the tool off the bench.[^hakko-fx888d-safety]

During manual soldering, flux smoke rises directly from the heated joint and enters the breathing zone. Rosin flux fume is associated with significant health hazards, notably occupational asthma. Use dedicated source extraction near the work, keep your head out of the rising plume, and avoid overheating the iron. A conventional desk fan that merely disperses smoke throughout the room is not equivalent to local exhaust capture.[^hse-solder-fume-indg248]

Eye protection is required whenever operations pose hazards from flying particles, molten solder splashes, or liquid chemicals; for instance, clipping component leads can propel cutoff fragments toward your face. Follow safety instructions for both flux and solder: residues and hazards vary depending on chemical formulation. When working with leaded alloys, avoid eating or drinking at the bench, clean up dross and residues properly, and wash hands before taking breaks.[^osha-eye-protection] [^hse-soldering-controls]

Example: before soldering, verify that the station is stable, the iron rests securely in its stand, and the fume extractor captures fumes at the joint. After finishing, switch off power, allow the iron to cool per the manufacturer instructions, and clean the work area.[^hakko-fx888d-safety] [^hse-solder-fume-indg248]

**Common mistakes:**
- Allowing fumes to drift across your face or assuming brief tasks do not warrant fume extraction.
- Leaving a hot iron lying on the bench without a stand.
- Handling food or drinks before washing hands after working with leaded solder.
