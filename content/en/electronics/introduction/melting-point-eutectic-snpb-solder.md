## Short answer

Eutectic 63/37 SnPb solder melts at 183 °C, with manufacturers specifying identical solidus and liquidus temperatures. Unlike non-eutectic alloys with a wide melting range, it transitions directly between solid and liquid without an intermediate pasty state.[^lucas-milhaupt-sn63pb37]

## Detailed explanation

Eutectic Sn63/Pb37 solder has identical solidus and liquidus temperatures of 183 °C, meaning it melts at a single temperature without the pasty transition range characteristic of non-eutectic alloys.[^lucas-milhaupt-sn63pb37] This is the temperature of the alloy itself, not the recommended temperature of the soldering iron tip.

A eutectic composition is one where the liquid phase transitions directly to solid at the lowest melting point for that alloy system. At the melting point, liquid and solid phases can coexist in equilibrium, but the alloy lacks an extended two-phase pasty interval. The joint transitions rapidly between solid and liquid states; however, joint quality still relies on wetting, flux activity, surface cleanliness, and proper thermal profiling.[^lucas-milhaupt-sn63pb37]

The designation 63/37 indicates nominally 63% tin and 37% lead. Other SnPb ratios (such as 60/40) have a plastic melting range, so 183 °C cannot be assumed for arbitrary tin-lead alloys.[^lucas-milhaupt-sn63pb37]

**Common mistake:** referring to 183 °C as the iron temperature. This is the melting point of the alloy; the soldering tip is set higher to compensate for heat sinking and adequately warm the joint, with the exact setting depending on the process and components.

Example: when the temperature inside the joint reaches 183 °C, this alloy melts. The manufacturer lists identical 183 °C values for both solidus and liquidus, confirming the absence of an interval between the onset and completion of melting.[^lucas-milhaupt-sn63pb37]
