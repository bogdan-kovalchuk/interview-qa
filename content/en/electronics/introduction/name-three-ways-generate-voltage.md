## Short answer

Voltage can be generated as a result of electrochemical reactions in a battery, electromagnetic induction in a generator, and the photovoltaic effect in a solar cell.[^openstax-electromagnetic-induction][^doe-solar-cell-basics] In a photovoltaic cell, absorbed light generates charge carriers that are separated within the semiconductor structure; this is not simply knocking electrons "out of the P-N junction".[^doe-solar-cell-basics]

## Detailed explanation

Voltage is the electrical potential difference between two points. A source creates it by separating charges or maintaining their separation through distinct physical processes. Three common examples are an electrochemical battery, a generator, and a solar cell; this is not an exhaustive list, as thermoelectric and piezoelectric sources also exist.[^openstax-electromagnetic-induction][^doe-solar-cell-basics]

In a battery, chemical reactions at the electrodes create a potential difference. During discharge, chemical energy is converted into electrical energy, while electrons travel through the external circuit; a secondary battery differs in that its chemical state can be restored by recharging under allowable conditions.[^aac-direct-current]

In a generator, the relative motion of a conductor and a magnetic field changes the magnetic flux through a circuit loop. According to Faraday's law, this change induces an electromotive force; if the circuit is closed, it can produce a current. A transformer also operates via time-varying magnetic flux, but transfers energy between windings rather than creating it from mechanical work.[^openstax-electromagnetic-induction]

In a photovoltaic cell, the semiconductor absorbs photons whose energy can create mobile electrons and holes. The internal electric field of the structure separates these charge carriers, while contacts allow delivering voltage and current to an external circuit. Therefore, the common phrasing that photons "knock electrons out of the P-N junction" is inaccurate: light excites carriers in the material, and the internal field facilitates their separation.[^doe-solar-cell-basics]

Example: rotating a coil in a magnetic field changes the magnetic flux through it, and a voltage appears across its terminals. If the magnet and coil remain stationary relative to each other and unchanging, the flux does not change and no such induced voltage appears; relative motion or other flux variation is the essential condition.[^openstax-electromagnetic-induction]
