## Short answer

The section covers mechanical switches and potentiometers, capacitors, inductors, RC/RL circuits, AC analysis, reactance, phasors, and complex impedance. It then addresses filters, diodes, power supplies, transistor stages, digital logic, clock oscillators, and the 555 timer; this reflects the outline of this specific course rather than a complete electrical engineering curriculum.[^udemy-course-section-4-outline]

## Detailed explanation

Section 4 is a major part of the specific course *Crash Course Electronics and PCB Design*, rather than a standardized topic outline for all electrical engineering courses. Its published syllabus begins with mechanical components and switches, then advances to potentiometers, capacitors, AC coupling, and RC and RL circuits.[^udemy-course-section-4-outline]

Topics on inductors cover magnetic fields, inductance, and the behavior of coils in circuits. Following RC/RL transient analysis, the course introduces current and voltage phase relationships, the ELI/ICE mnemonics, reactance, phasors, and complex impedance. This reflects a pedagogical sequence: not every calculation demands the full suite of mathematical tools, but they are essential for analyzing sinusoidal circuits with reactive components.[^udemy-course-section-4-outline] [^aac-alternating-current]

The curriculum then applies these concepts to low-pass and high-pass filters, moving on to diodes, rectifiers, voltage regulation, and power supplies. Subsequent units examine transistors as switches and amplifiers, followed by digital logic, clock generation circuits, crystal oscillators, and the 555 timer.[^udemy-course-section-4-outline] [^aac-semiconductors]
