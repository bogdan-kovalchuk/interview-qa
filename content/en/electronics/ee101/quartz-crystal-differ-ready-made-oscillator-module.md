## Short answer

A quartz crystal resonator sets the frequency, but oscillation requires an active feedback amplifier such as an inverter in a Pierce configuration.[^ti-sn74lvc1gx04-oscillator] A ready-made oscillator module typically outputs a logic-level clock signal (such as TTL or HCMOS), though sine-wave outputs also exist.[^abracon-quartz-glossary] An enable pin, if present, controls the output state: grounding it places the output into a high-impedance state.[^abracon-quartz-glossary]

## Detailed explanation

A quartz crystal resonator is a frequency-determining component: it determines the frequency at which oscillation can occur, but on its own it does not produce a logic clock signal. Oscillations are generated by the circuit surrounding the resonator, not by the resonator itself. In TI's example for a Pierce oscillator, an inverter amplifies the signal, resistor R_F biases the inverter into its linear operating region, and the values of C1 and C2 are selected to compute crystal load capacitance C_L from them.[^ti-sn74lvc1gx04-oscillator]

A ready-made oscillator module is a self-contained unit: the crystal and the oscillator circuit are already integrated inside the package, and power, ground, the output, and in many modules an enable pin are brought out. The manufacturer describes the output as conforming to specified logic states, such as TTL or HCMOS; sine-wave outputs also exist.[^abracon-quartz-glossary] Therefore, the designer does not select C1, C2, and R_F, but takes the frequency, logic levels, and output type directly from the module's specification.

The enable pin is among the parameters that must be verified in the datasheet, because its presence and polarity depend on the specific product. In Abracon's description, grounding the input places the output into a high-impedance state, whereas an input left open or driven to a logic high allows normal oscillation.[^abracon-quartz-glossary] This is the definition of one manufacturer and cannot be assumed for other modules without verification.

Choosing between a discrete crystal and an oscillator module comes down to who is responsible for the oscillator circuit. With a discrete crystal, it is the designer: they select the inverter, load, and feedback, and verify startup at the lowest supply voltage.[^ti-sn74lvc1gx04-oscillator] In a module, these decisions are embedded by the manufacturer, and the designer receives a ready-made clock output.

**Common mistakes:**

- Assuming that a quartz crystal resonator produces a logic clock signal on its own without an inverter circuit;
- Connecting a module's enable pin without checking its polarity: for Abracon, a grounded input places the output into a high-impedance state;[^abracon-quartz-glossary]
- Assuming the enable logic of one module applies to another, even when the pin name is identical.
