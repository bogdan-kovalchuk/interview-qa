## Short answer

A linear regulator adjusts pass-element resistance so that `V_in - V_out` is dissipated as heat: it is simple and low-noise, but inefficient across large voltage drops.[^ti-slva118a][^ti-slyt527] An LDO is a linear regulator with low dropout voltage, but requires careful adherence to datasheet output capacitor constraints.[^ti-slva118a][^ti-slva079] A switching converter cycles switches with inductors: efficiency is significantly higher, but it introduces ripple, EMI, and complex design.[^ti-slyt527][^ti-switching-regulator-fundamentals] A charge pump switches capacitors without inductors for modest currents and integer ratios like ≈ `2*V_in` or `-V_in`.[^ti-charge-pump-basics]

## Detailed explanation

A linear regulator uses a pass element controlled by feedback circuitry to hold the output steady; it is convenient to envision it as a potentiometer between input and output. Input current approximately equals output current, so the voltage difference is converted entirely into heat: `P_D ≈ (V_in - V_out)*I_out`, and efficiency is ≈ `V_out/V_in`.[^ti-slva118a] This highlights its limitation: a linear regulator can only step down voltage, and it is inefficient across large voltage drops. On the other hand, the circuit is simple (requiring few components) and produces very low ripple, which is why linear regulators are often placed after switching converters to clean up the output.[^ti-slyt527]

An LDO shares the same architecture but features low dropout – that is, a small minimum `V_in - V_out` difference at which the regulator still operates within specification. This offers an advantage when the input is close to the output or when operating from a battery.[^ti-slva118a] In the dropout region, a PMOS pass element acts simply as a resistor, `V_dropout = I_o*R_on`.[^ti-slva079] The trade-off is stricter stability requirements: manufacturers specify an acceptable equivalent series resistance (ESR) range for the output capacitor (0.2–9 Ω for the TPS763xx in the application note), so arbitrarily swapping capacitors risks instability.[^ti-slva079] Low dropout does not alter the underlying physics: across a large voltage drop, an LDO dissipates `(V_in - V_out)*I_out` just like a standard linear regulator.[^ti-slva118a]

A switching converter operates differently: a switch periodically connects the input to an inductor; inductor current cannot change instantaneously and continues to flow through a diode after the switch opens, while the output is regulated by pulse-width modulation (PWM).[^ti-switching-regulator-fundamentals] The voltage difference is not dropped across a lossy pass element, so efficiency is typically much higher, and various topologies can step down, step up, or invert voltages.[^ti-switching-regulator-fundamentals] The cost is ripple, EMI generated by rapid switching, and more complex design and PCB layout.[^ti-switching-regulator-fundamentals] In TI's example with a 24 V input, 5 V output, and 100 mA load, a linear regulator dissipates `P_D ≈ (24 - 5)*0.1 = 1.9 W` with an efficiency of `5/24 ≈ 21%`; measured values on that board were about 20% efficiency and 2 W dissipation versus 84.5% and 0.093 W for a buck converter, with output ripple under 10 mV compared to 75 mV.[^ti-slyt527]

A charge pump uses a "flying" capacitor instead of an inductor: it charges to `V_in` and is then switched in series with the input, producing an output of approximately `2*V_in` (doubler) or `-V_in` (inverter), such as ≈ 10 V or ≈ -5 V from a 5 V input.[^ti-charge-pump-basics] An unregulated charge pump does not regulate the output, while a regulated doubler only regulates between `V_in` and `2*V_in`. By TI's assessment, charge pumps are well suited for tens of milliamperes, perform poorly above 250 mA, and are less efficient than inductive converters unless run open-loop without regulation.[^ti-charge-pump-basics]

**Common mistakes:**

- Assuming an LDO is "efficient" at any input voltage: the `(V_in - V_out)*I_out` loss remains; only the minimum required headroom is lower.
- Installing an arbitrary output capacitor on an LDO without verifying the acceptable ESR range in the datasheet.
- Placing a switching converter before sensitive analog circuitry without filtering or a linear post-regulator.
- Selecting a charge pump for heavy load currents: it is designed for modest loads.
